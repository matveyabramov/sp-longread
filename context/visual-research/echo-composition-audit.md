# Echo composition audit

## 1. The page has margins, but not a governing editorial grid

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — Most sections share the same 64 px outer inset, but their internal axes are independently improvised with `38%`, `40%`, `43%`, `13%`, `12%` gaps and unrelated column ratios. The title, body, media, indices and conclusions do not repeatedly register to a small set of recognizable coordinates (`src/style.css:3`, `src/style.css:31–35`; `index.html:25–39`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The page looks consistently padded rather than deliberately composed. Each block is locally tidy, but the eye cannot feel a stable editorial skeleton carrying different scenes.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Establish one desktop grid with the current 64 px outer margin and 12 columns, then define three recurring content anchors: left/title, middle/object, right/reading-copy. Rebuild Brain, Elephant, Echo, Next Viewing and Final against those exact anchors; allow sections to span different column combinations instead of introducing new percentage margins per section. On mobile, replace this with a deliberate two-axis composition rather than proportional shrinkage.
4. **SCOPE** — GLOBAL
5. **PRIORITY** — critical

## 2. Section rhythm is still generated by one shared shell

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — The global `.section` applies essentially the same 112 px vertical padding to every chapter, reduced uniformly to 65 px on mobile; the same ruled `.section-label` opens nearly every section (`src/style.css:3`, `src/style.css:5–6`; `index.html:23–39`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — Chapters read as modules in a styled article template. Sparse conceptual scenes, dense taxonomies and climaxes arrive with the same entrance, so content quantity—not art direction—determines their perceived duration.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Define section-specific scenic heights: keep Blind Spot quiet and near-viewport-sized; make Brain one contained perceptual field; keep Blind Zones compact; let Film Cases run long behind a held media plane; make 98% a single desktop climax; give Final a calm near-viewport ending. Retain the chapter label content, but vary its placement and remove the full-width top rule where another boundary already defines the scene.
4. **SCOPE** — GLOBAL
5. **PRIORITY** — critical

## 3. Hero has strong ingredients, but the green slab wins over the TV-head

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — The desktop render gives the question almost the full 1152 px content width, with the acid slab forming the loudest object. The TV-head reads as a large atmospheric background crop rather than the singular visual anchor promised by the direction (`index.html:16–20`; `src/style.css:67–78`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The composition demonstrates “large type over a hero image,” but the type and media do not feel mutually dependent. The image could be replaced with another cinematic still without changing the geometry.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Enlarge and shift the TV-head so the screen and head form an unmistakable 45–60% visual mass; reserve a clean pocket around the screen. Constrain the first question line to the left/title axis and let the second line cross toward the image without spanning nearly the entire viewport. Keep the current mobile crop, which already produces a stronger object/type relationship than desktop.
4. **SCOPE** — Hero
5. **PRIORITY** — high

## 4. The rectangular slab has become a section template instead of a selective device

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — Paper, acid or dark slabs appear in Hero, Brain, Elephant, Film Cases, Echo and Next Viewing, usually as the final line of a large heading (`index.html:19`, `index.html:25`, `index.html:27`, `index.html:31`, `index.html:35`, `index.html:37`; `src/style.css:57–65`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — Repetition is attached to the same syntactic position rather than to a semantic role. After several sections, the slab reads as a reusable heading component—the exact signal of a well-styled prototype—rather than an editorial intervention.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Reserve slabs for three explicit roles only: the Hero question, one thesis/reveal in Elephant, and an active phrase over moving media in Film Cases. Remove the slab from Brain, Echo and Next Viewing; give those chapters distinct static devices based on absence/completion, mismatch and sequence respectively.
4. **SCOPE** — GLOBAL
5. **PRIORITY** — high

## 5. Brain explains the idea but does not stage “completion of an absence”

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — Brain is a conventional two-column heading/body layout followed by a centered sentence strip. Its three parts stack as heading, explanation and diagram-like summary (`index.html:25`; `src/style.css:3`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — Nothing is dominant except the heading, and the strip looks like a wireframe explainer component. The composition does not make the reader perceive a missing field being completed; it only states the idea in text.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Redesign the section as one stable two-plane scene: a 360–440 px reading column with the caveat attached, opposed by a large empty/perceptual field that contains the “Слова знакомы → Смысл? → Сюжет продолжается” logic spatially. Reverse the reading axis relative to the experiment and keep the title subordinate to the field instead of filling half the viewport.
4. **SCOPE** — Brain
5. **PRIORITY** — critical

## 6. Elephant separates its three potential protagonists instead of composing them together

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — On desktop, the Russian section heading occupies the upper scene, the English phrase begins near the lower-right edge, and the elephant arrives largely below the first viewport. The object is confined to a 45% rectangular crop, while a second large heading competes above it (`index.html:27`; `src/style.css:31–35`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The section reads as “heading, then illustration/phrase module, then four rows.” The elephant is content inside a stage, not an object intruding into language; the intended idiom collision is therefore absent from the resting composition.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Compositionally merge the elephant and English phrase into one desktop stage visible at arrival: object at roughly 35–45% width, phrase on the middle/right anchor, elephant contour crossing the phrase’s surrounding space without covering words. Reduce the Russian heading’s scale or move it to a margin role so it does not compete. Start the four explanatory rows only after this object/phrase composition has fully resolved.
4. **SCOPE** — Elephant
5. **PRIORITY** — critical

## 7. Blind Zones is a clean table, not yet a typographic chapter

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — Four categories use identical ruled rows, identical alignment and identical weight. At 1280 px the labels render at about 51 px while explanations sit at 14 px; the hidden section heading leaves no visual thesis object (`index.html:29`; `src/style.css:3`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The section resembles an index or feature matrix. Equal rows communicate classification efficiently, but not the dense change of pace or extreme tiny-index/dominant-type contrast called for by the primary reference.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Keep all four categories in one compact scene, but size the system from “Cultural reference” first and raise desktop labels into a tested 64–80 px range. Register labels, 240–320 px explanations and tiny indices to different grid anchors; alternate the explanation side or baseline in a controlled two-by-two rhythm instead of repeating one row template. Do not add cards or imagery.
4. **SCOPE** — Blind Zones
5. **PRIORITY** — high

## 8. Film Cases has no dominant visual object in its default and mobile states

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — The full-viewport media plane is invisible until a phrase receives hover/focus. Desktop therefore opens on a flat dark list, and mobile—where hover is absent—reads almost entirely as oversized text rows before click opens the detail dialog (`index.html:31`; `src/main.js:101–124`, `src/main.js:155–163`; `src/style.css:36–49`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The strongest art-directed relationship exists only as an interaction state. The static composition, especially on mobile, looks like a component inventory and fails the requirement that an important section own a dominant visual object.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Give the section an intentional initial selection: load a representative still/frame for case 01 as soon as the chapter enters view, with the first phrase visually active but still clickable. On mobile, keep a persistent cropped poster/still plane behind or above the active phrase list rather than relying on hover; clicking may still open the existing dialog. Preserve the current full-bleed treatment and media swap structure.
4. **SCOPE** — Film Cases
5. **PRIORITY** — critical

## 9. The 98% climax disconnects its qualifier from the number on desktop

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — At 1280 × 720 the 435 px number occupies almost the entire visible scene; the statement and scientific qualification fall below the fold (`index.html:33`; `src/style.css:3`). On mobile the relationship is clearer because all elements fit in one vertical composition.
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The number is visually impressive but semantically ambiguous at the moment of impact. The reader first sees a statistic-like poster and only later receives the text that prevents the exact misreading the brief warns against.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Keep `98%` whole and huge, but build one desktop stage in which the qualifier is visible simultaneously: anchor the number to the lower-left/baseline of the scene and place the main statement plus compact caveat in a narrow right column or in the negative space beside the percent sign. Reduce top dead space rather than reducing the number into an ordinary heading.
4. **SCOPE** — 98%
5. **PRIORITY** — critical

## 10. Echo turns the story’s key insight into another heading followed by ruled rows

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — Echo repeats the established large heading/slab treatment, then presents three expectation/reaction pairs in uniform horizontal rows, followed by a centered conclusion (`index.html:35`; `src/style.css:3`, `src/style.css:16`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — This is the conceptual payoff of the title, yet it uses the page’s most generic content pattern. The mismatch between “what I understood” and “what the scene did” is described but never made spatially visible.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Redesign the three pairs as one field of displaced alignments: quiet expectations locked to the left anchor, reactions offset on the right at visibly different vertical positions, with a deliberate void between them. Let the conclusion bridge or align those two axes. Remove the acid slab here so the relationship—not a highlighted heading fragment—is the dominant object.
4. **SCOPE** — Echo
5. **PRIORITY** — critical

## 11. Next Viewing is visually heavier and more instructional than its role requires

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — A large two-line slab heading is followed by a left paragraph and right numbered list. On mobile, the slab wraps into three dense lines above two stacked instruction blocks (`index.html:37`; `src/style.css:3`, `src/style.css:6`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The section resembles a polished how-to module and repeats the same thesis-heading device used earlier. It does not provide the requested quiet editorial spread after the Echo payoff.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Remove the paper slab and lower the heading’s mass. On desktop, set the framing sentence as a narrow margin note and place “Во время просмотра” and “После” as two spacious editorial columns sharing one baseline. On mobile, preserve the two phases but shorten line lengths and add more separation between them instead of making the heading the largest object in the chapter.
4. **SCOPE** — Next Viewing
5. **PRIORITY** — high

## 12. Final uses the right motif but leaves the dot decoratively detached

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — The final scene is a large multiline sentence inset by 13%, with a small green dot parked to its left and a return link below (`index.html:39`; `src/style.css:3`). Mobile turns this into dot, paragraph and link in a standard vertical stack.
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The dot references the experiment but does not change the sentence’s composition or meaning. The ending therefore feels like a final quote template with a brand marker, not a visual rhyme that resolves the blind-spot metaphor.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Give the dot a precise coordinate related to the experiment and compose the sentence around an intentional empty third of the viewport. Use the muted phrase as the transition across that void, then let “понятным” land on a separate anchor. Keep roughly one-third of the final scene free of text and make the credits begin as a clearly separate, smaller plane.
4. **SCOPE** — Final
5. **PRIORITY** — high

## 13. Display typography is scaled up, but not differentiated enough by role

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — One Golos Text family carries brand, giant headings, body, metadata, English phrases and the 98% figure. Most display text also shares very tight tracking around −5.5% to −6.5%, while several large editorial headings are forced to a relatively loose `1.18` line-height (`src/style.css:1`, `src/style.css:63–65`, `src/style.css:71–77`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — Scale changes, but voice changes less than it should. The same grotesk texture and the same compressed tracking make distinct chapters feel like variants of one component; the loose line-height then separates multi-line Russian headings into stacked lines rather than one shaped typographic object.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Keep Golos Text for body and metadata, but introduce or designate one genuinely expressive display face for only the largest Russian headings, case phrases and 98%. Build three explicit display roles with tested Russian metrics: compact headline around `0.96–1.05` line-height, large phrase around `0.98–1.08`, and reading display around `1.1–1.2`. Ease tracking separately for Cyrillic and Latin instead of applying approximately −5.5% everywhere; use balanced wrapping for headings and verify every forced `<br>` at 1280, 1440 and 390 px.
4. **SCOPE** — GLOBAL
5. **PRIORITY** — high

## 14. Secondary text becomes small and faint instead of intentionally subordinate

1. **CURRENT PROBLEM** — Metadata and indices render at 8–11 px in several desktop/mobile states; layer explanations fall to 14–15 px, while muted text is repeatedly used for whole explanatory blocks (`src/style.css:3`, `src/style.css:5–6`, `src/style.css:45–51`; `index.html:27–37`).
2. **WHY IT FEELS WEAK / PROTOTYPE-LIKE** — The hierarchy depends on making support text recede, but some text drops below a comfortable editorial reading size. This creates the visual signature of placeholder captions around oversized headings rather than premium microtypography.
3. **SPECIFIC CHANGE** — Set a real floor: 16–18 px for explanatory prose, 13–14 px for captions/instructions, and 11–12 px only for short indices. Keep uppercase metadata on one line where possible, reduce its tracking on mobile, and constrain prose to roughly 60–75 characters per line. Use color sparingly for hierarchy after size and measure are correct.
4. **SCOPE** — GLOBAL
5. **PRIORITY** — high

## The 5 changes that would create the largest visual improvement

1. Establish one explicit grid with three recurring anchors, then recompose sections against it.
2. Redesign Brain and Echo as conceptual spatial fields rather than heading/body/row modules.
3. Merge the elephant, phrase and section title into one visible object/type composition.
4. Make Film Cases media present by default—including on mobile—so the chapter always has a dominant visual plane.
5. Rebuild the desktop 98% scene so the number and its qualifier are visible as one typographic climax.
